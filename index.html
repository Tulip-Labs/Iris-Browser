Iris for Linux and Android

A browser made of glass.

Liquid Glass that bends the live page, Shields that stop ads and trackers inside the engine, and nothing about you ever sent anywhere. Free and open source, by Tulip Labs.

Features

Clear by design.

Iris for Linux is written in Odin and talks to Wayland, OpenGL ES and WPE WebKit directly. No GTK, no X11. Every piece of its chrome is drawn by Iris itself, in the same frame as its glass.

In Iris today

  • Real Liquid Glass

    Not frosted glass: a shader lens that bends the live page at its rim, with a touch of colour splitting and a line of light along the top.

  • Containers

    You make your own, each with its own colour. Each keeps its own cookies, storage, cache and sign-ins.

  • An automatic sandbox

    Sites Iris does not trust (plain http, a bare IP, a failing certificate) open in a world of their own, with none of your data.

  • Ads open no tabs

    A pop-up needs your click and goes only to the same site or a sign-in page. Anything else is stopped, with an Open button if you meant it.

  • YouTube without ads

    The player's answer loses its ads before the player reads it, so no ad is scheduled and no request is blocked for YouTube to notice.

  • Sharp at every scale

    Wayland's fractional scaling: pages and chrome render at the screen's own pixels, at 1.25, 1.5 or 2.

Coming in 2.3 In progress

  • A start page drawn by Iris

    The glass ring and one centered search box, drawn in Odin. No tracking tiles.

  • The full address, centered

    The address bar sits in the middle and shows the whole address, so you always know where you are.

Privacy

Shields up. Nothing out.

Iris collects nothing. There is no account, no telemetry, no analytics and no crash reporting, and Tulip Labs runs no servers for Iris.

Shields stop ads and trackers inside the engine, before a request leaves your device, using uBlock Origin's and EasyList's lists, with a built-in list of ad, tracker, fingerprinting and mining domains. Third-party cookies are refused, and you can turn Shields off for one site.

Shields levels

Moderate
Ads and trackers from other sites (EasyList, EasyPrivacy). A site's own requests always load.
Aggressive
Also a site's own ads and trackers, uBlock Origin's filters, and the empty boxes ads leave. The default in Iris 2.2.
Ultra aggressive
Also cookie notices, newsletter and sign-up pop-ups and other annoyances. Coming in 2.3: the default, and tracking parameters are stripped from links.

Two engines

One Iris. Your choice of engine.

The same Odin app, the same glass, built on either engine. Both Flatpaks install side by side.

Default

WPE WebKit

Each page frame arrives as a DMA-BUF and Iris samples it with no copy, so the glass bends the live page. WebKit keeps its own process sandbox inside Flatpak's.

io.github.tulip_labs.Iris

Chromium (CEF)

"Iris (Chromium)", built on CEF as a Flatpak of its own, for when you want Chromium underneath. It is larger, has no proprietary codecs, and runs its renderers inside Flatpak's sandbox only.

Android

The same glass in your pocket.

Iris for Android is Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, drawn as Liquid Glass with no Material components, on Cefrium: its own Chromium, the same engine on every phone.

  • Shields at the same three levels, and ads that cannot open tabs
  • Containers, each with its own cookies, cache and storage
  • Audio that keeps playing with the screen off
  • A downloads manager that needs no storage permission
  • Updates itself from Iris's own GitHub releases

Install

Get Iris.

Both builds come from GitHub Releases. The docs cover updates, the Chromium build and building from source.

Linux

  1. Download Iris-x86_64.flatpak from the latest release.
  2. Iris needs the GNOME 50 runtime, which Flatpak installs from Flathub. If Flathub isn't set up yet: 
    flatpak remote-add --if-not-exists --user flathub https://dl.flathub.org/repo/flathub.flatpakrepo
  3. Install the bundle: 
    flatpak install --user Iris-x86_64.flatpak

On niri 26.04 and newer, the blur behind the window comes on by itself.

Download the Flatpak

Android

  1. Download Iris-android.apk from the latest release.
  2. Open it and let Android install it. It needs Android 10 or newer.
  3. That's all: from then on, Iris updates itself.

FAQ

Questions.

Is Iris free?

Yes. Iris is free software under the GNU General Public License 3.0, and its source is on GitHub.

What does Iris collect about me?

Nothing. Your history, tabs, cookies and settings stay on your device. Iris asks GitHub once a day whether there's a newer release, and that request carries nothing about you beyond your network address. The privacy page has the details.

Which Linux desktops does it run on?

Iris for Linux is a native Wayland app: it has no X11 or GTK code. It runs on Wayland compositors; where the compositor offers background blur (niri 26.04 and newer), the desktop shows through the glass.

What happened to the apt, dnf and zypper repositories?

They are discontinued. Iris for Linux ships as a Flatpak; please install it from GitHub Releases as shown above.

Why the name?

Iris is the Greek goddess of the rainbow, who carries light between worlds. The icon is a clear glass ring, light entering and leaving as a rainbow.
